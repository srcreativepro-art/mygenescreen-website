5 Reasons to See a Genetic Counselor Before Pregnancy in India

By Lakshita Chauhan Thakore, CGC — BGCI Certified Genetic Counselor, Ahmedabad  |  August 2026

Most couples think about genetic testing only after a pregnancy complication — an abnormal ultrasound, a positive NIPT result, or a child born with a condition. But preconception genetic counseling, the guidance you receive before you get pregnant, is often far more powerful. It gives you options before they become decisions made under stress.

As a BGCI Certified Genetic Counselor in Ahmedabad who has counselled thousands of families across India, I see the same pattern: couples wish they had come earlier. Here are the five most important reasons to see a genetic counselor before your pregnancy — and who specifically needs it most.

Who Is Preconception Genetic Counseling For?

While anyone planning a pregnancy can benefit, it is especially recommended for:

Couples with a family history of any genetic condition
Couples from the same community or caste (consanguinity risk)
Couples of Gujarati, Sindhi, or other at-risk ethnic backgrounds (thalassemia)
Anyone with a previous pregnancy loss or child with a birth defect
Couples pursuing IVF or PGT (preimplantation genetic testing)
Women over 35 planning their first pregnancy
Couples who are blood relatives (first or second cousins)
Anyone who has had a child with chromosomal or genetic condition

The 5 Reasons

1

You Can Find Out If You're a Carrier — Before It Matters

Carrier screening is the most important — and most underutilised — genetic test available before pregnancy. A carrier is someone who carries one changed copy of a recessive gene but has no symptoms themselves. If both parents are carriers of the same condition, each pregnancy has a 25% chance of being affected.

In India, the most important carriers to screen for include:

  • Thalassemia — Extremely common in Gujarat, Sindh, Punjab, and coastal states. If both parents are carriers, a child has a 25% chance of thalassemia major — a severe, lifelong condition requiring regular blood transfusions.
  • Sickle Cell Disease — High frequency in tribal populations across Gujarat, Maharashtra, Odisha, and central India.
  • Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA) — 1 in 40–50 people carry an SMN1 deletion; now treatable with early intervention.
  • Cystic Fibrosis, Fragile X — Less common but clinically significant.

Carrier screening before pregnancy means you have time to make informed decisions: proceed naturally with monitoring, pursue IVF with PGT (preimplantation genetic diagnosis) to select unaffected embryos, or use donor gametes. After a pregnancy, your options narrow significantly.

2

You Can Get a Personalised Risk Assessment Based on Your Family History

Your family history is the richest genetic dataset you possess — and most people have never had it properly analysed. A genetic counselor builds a 3-generation pedigree (family tree) and identifies patterns of inheritance: autosomal dominant conditions (where one copy of a gene causes disease), X-linked conditions, and multifactorial risks.

Common findings that emerge from pedigree analysis:

  • Multiple family members with early-onset heart disease → hereditary cardiomyopathy or channelopathy risk
  • Several women with breast or ovarian cancer → BRCA1/2 screening for you and your future child
  • A sibling or cousin with intellectual disability → risk of X-linked or recessive condition in your own children
  • A parent with early-onset neurological disease → Huntington's, FRDA, or mitochondrial disorder risk

Many of these are actionable before pregnancy — allowing targeted testing, lifestyle modification, or specialist referral.

3

You Can Avoid the Anxiety of an Unexpected Prenatal Finding

Prenatal testing — NIPT (non-invasive prenatal testing), anomaly scans, amniocentesis — is now routinely offered to pregnant women across India. These tests detect chromosomal conditions, structural anomalies, and some genetic disorders in the developing baby.

However, receiving an unexpected abnormal result during pregnancy — when you are already emotionally invested — is vastly more stressful than understanding your risks in advance. Preconception counseling allows you to:

  • Understand which prenatal tests are relevant for your risk profile
  • Know in advance what a "positive" result would mean and what your options are
  • Decide ahead of time — calmly — how you would respond to different findings
  • Avoid unnecessary testing driven by anxiety rather than clinical indication

Couples who have had preconception counseling navigate prenatal findings with significantly less distress — because they have already thought through the possibilities.

4

If You're Pursuing IVF, Genetic Counseling Is Essential

Preimplantation Genetic Testing (PGT) — genetic testing of embryos before transfer — is one of the most powerful tools available to couples at risk of having a child with a genetic condition. It requires preconception genetic counseling to be done correctly.

PGT comes in three forms:

  • PGT-A — Tests embryos for chromosomal number abnormalities (aneuploidy). Relevant for recurrent miscarriage and advanced maternal age.
  • PGT-M — Tests embryos for a specific single-gene disorder (e.g., thalassemia, SMA, DMD). Requires genetic counseling and a specific probe to be designed before the IVF cycle.
  • PGT-SR — For couples where one partner carries a chromosomal rearrangement (e.g., translocation).

Preconception counseling ensures PGT-M is set up correctly, your carrier status is confirmed, and the probe is ready before egg retrieval — saving you precious time and IVF cycles.

5

You Get Culturally Sensitive Guidance From Someone Who Understands India

Genetic counseling in India has unique dimensions that Western guidelines don't always address: extended family expectations, consanguineous marriages, community-specific carrier frequencies (Gujarati thalassemia rates, tribal sickle cell prevalence), and the role of elders in reproductive decisions.

A BGCI-certified genetic counselor trained and practising in India understands:

  • The high carrier frequency of specific conditions in your community
  • How to discuss findings with extended family in a culturally appropriate way
  • The Indian legal framework around prenatal diagnosis (PCPNDT Act limitations)
  • Practical access to PGT, carrier testing, and prenatal diagnosis across India
  • Emotional and family dynamics specific to the Indian context

This is not something a genetic report from an international lab — or a quick online search — can provide.

Prenatal vs Preconception Counseling — What's the Difference?

Preconception counseling happens before pregnancy. It identifies your risks, allows carrier screening, and gives you the full range of reproductive options.

Prenatal counseling happens during pregnancy — usually triggered by an abnormal scan, a positive NIPT, or a known family history. Options are still available, but the time pressure and emotional weight are much greater.

Both are valuable. But preconception counseling, when available, is always preferable.

Frequently Asked Questions

How early before pregnancy should I see a genetic counselor?

Ideally 3–6 months before you plan to start trying. This gives enough time for carrier testing results, specialist referrals if needed, and PGT probe preparation if IVF is being considered.

Is preconception genetic counseling covered by insurance in India?

Most Indian health insurance plans do not cover standalone genetic counseling sessions, though some corporate plans do. Carrier screening tests may be partially covered. Check with your insurer. The consultation fee at MyGeneScreen Clinic is ₹1,500 — significantly less than the cost of a single prenatal intervention later.

I'm Gujarati — do I specifically need thalassemia carrier screening?

Yes. Gujarat has one of the highest beta-thalassemia carrier rates in India — estimated at 4–10% of the population in some communities. If both partners are carriers, each pregnancy has a 25% risk of thalassemia major. Carrier screening takes a single blood test and provides certainty. It should be done before any planned pregnancy in Gujarat.

We're cousins — is our child at higher risk?

First-cousin marriages increase the risk of autosomal recessive conditions (where both parents must be carriers) by 4–7 times compared to the general population. This risk is manageable with expanded carrier screening and appropriate prenatal monitoring. Consanguinity itself does not mean a child will be affected — but it does make preconception counseling especially important.
