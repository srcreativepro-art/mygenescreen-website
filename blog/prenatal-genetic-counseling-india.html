Most couples think about genetic testing only after a pregnancy complication — an abnormal ultrasound, a positive NIPT, or a child born with a condition. But preconception genetic counseling — the guidance you receive before you get pregnant — is often far more powerful. It gives you options before they become decisions made under stress.

As a BGCI Certified Genetic Counselor in Ahmedabad, I see the same pattern: couples wish they had come earlier. Here are the five most important reasons to see a genetic counselor before your pregnancy.

Who Is Preconception Genetic Counseling For?

While anyone planning a pregnancy can benefit, it is especially recommended for:

Couples with any family history of a genetic condition
Gujarati, Sindhi, or other communities with high carrier rates (thalassemia)
Couples from the same caste or community (consanguinity)
Previous pregnancy loss or child with a birth defect
Couples pursuing IVF or PGT
Women over 35 planning their first pregnancy
Blood relatives planning to marry or conceive
Anyone with a sibling or child with intellectual disability

The 5 Reasons

1
You Can Find Out If You're a Carrier — Before It Matters

A carrier is someone who carries one changed copy of a recessive gene but is completely healthy themselves. If both partners are carriers of the same condition, each pregnancy has a 25% chance of being affected.

In India, the most important conditions to screen for include:

  • Thalassemia — Extremely common in Gujarat, Sindh, Punjab. Both-carrier couples face 25% risk of thalassemia major per pregnancy.
  • Sickle Cell Disease — High in tribal populations across Gujarat, Maharashtra, Odisha, and central India.
  • Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA) — 1 in 40–50 people carry an SMN1 deletion; now treatable with early intervention.

Testing before pregnancy means you have time to choose: proceed naturally, pursue IVF with PGT (preimplantation genetic diagnosis), or use donor gametes. After a pregnancy starts, your options narrow significantly.

2
You Get a Personalised Risk Assessment From Your Family History

Your family history is the richest genetic dataset you possess — and most people have never had it properly analysed. A genetic counselor builds a 3-generation pedigree and identifies inheritance patterns that may put your child at risk.

Common findings that emerge from family history review:

  • Multiple family members with early-onset heart disease → hereditary cardiomyopathy risk
  • Several women with breast or ovarian cancer → BRCA1/2 screening indicated
  • A sibling with intellectual disability → possible X-linked or recessive condition
  • A parent with early-onset neurological disease → Huntington's or mitochondrial disorder risk
3
You Can Avoid the Anxiety of an Unexpected Prenatal Finding

NIPT, anomaly scans, and amniocentesis are routinely offered during pregnancy across India. Receiving an unexpected abnormal result when you are already emotionally invested is vastly more stressful than understanding your risks in advance.

Preconception counseling allows you to understand which tests are relevant for your specific risk profile, know in advance what a positive result would mean, and decide calmly — before pregnancy — how you would respond to different findings.

4
If You're Pursuing IVF, Genetic Counseling Is Essential

Preimplantation Genetic Testing (PGT) — genetic testing of embryos before transfer — requires preconception genetic counseling to be done correctly. PGT-M (for single-gene disorders like thalassemia or SMA) requires a custom probe designed 3–4 months before egg retrieval.

Without preconception counseling, couples often lose IVF cycles because PGT preparation wasn't started in time. A genetic counselor ensures everything is set up before your cycle begins.

5
You Get Culturally Sensitive Guidance From Someone Who Understands India

Genetic counseling in India has unique dimensions: consanguineous marriages, community-specific carrier frequencies, extended family expectations, and the role of elders in reproductive decisions. A BGCI-certified counselor practising in India understands:

  • High carrier frequencies specific to your community
  • How to discuss findings with extended family appropriately
  • The Indian legal framework around prenatal diagnosis
  • Practical access to PGT and carrier testing across India

Prenatal vs Preconception — What's the Difference?

Preconception counseling happens before pregnancy. It identifies your risks, allows carrier screening, and gives you the full range of reproductive options — including IVF with PGT if needed.

Prenatal counseling happens during pregnancy — triggered by an abnormal scan, a positive NIPT, or a known family history. Options are still available, but time pressure and emotional weight are much greater.

💡

Both are valuable. But preconception counseling, when available, is always preferable. Ideal timing: 3–6 months before you plan to start trying.
